# Doctrinal Sins: Grounding the Narrator's Theological Condemnations

## 0. Purpose and Method

The narrator of *DEUS VULT* speaks in a grave 12th-century chronicle register. When it names the
adversary's god a false god, it must do so the way the period's most literate churchmen did:
by **charging doctrine, not mocking people**. The difference matters. Robert of Ketton's 1143
Latin Quran was titled *Lex Mahumet pseudoprophete* — "the law of Mahomet the *pseudo*-prophet" —
a doctrinal verdict (false prophecy) rather than an ethnic sneer
([Quran12-21 project, *Bibliander edition*](https://quran12-21.org/en/contexts/bibliander);
[Wikipedia: Peter the Venerable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Venerable)).
Our register follows that line: **condemn propositions, pity souls, never caricature bodies,
habits, or speech.**

Every conflict point below cites the primary text on both sides. Scripture links use standard
references; Quran links use [Corpus Coranicum](https://corpuscoranicum.de/en) referencing the
Cairo 1924 edition; hadith uses Sahih Muslim.

## 1. Core Doctrinal Conflicts (Nicene Christian Standpoint)

### 1.0 A note on the sin-mapping discipline

The Seven Deadly Sins (Pride, Envy, Lust, Sloth, Wrath, Gluttony, Greed) are a medieval Western
schema, and the honest reader finds it maps **Christian** moral pathology far better than Islamic
juristic error. Most doctrinal disagreement is not a vice at all — it is *heresy* (*haeresis*) in
the medieval taxonomy, which the Scholastics treated as a species of unbelief adjacent to, but
distinct from, the capital sins. Below, each point is mapped **only where the mapping is
historically and theologically defensible**. Where it is not, we say "unmappable — classify as
Idolatry/Untruth" and the game should use its doctrinal-error tags instead of a sin tag. Forcing
a mapping produces exactly the cheap writing this document exists to prevent.

### 1.1 Denial of the Trinity and the Sonship of Christ

- **Islamic text:** "O People of the Scripture, do not commit excess in your religion or say about
  Allah except the truth… the Messiah, Jesus, the son of Mary, was but a messenger of Allah…
  Allah is one god. Exalted is He above having a son" ([Q 4:171](https://corpuscoranicum.de/).
  See also [Q 5:72-75](https://corpuscoranicum.de/) ("they have certainly disbelieved who say,
  'Allah is the Messiah'"), [Q 9:30](https://corpuscoranicum.de/) ("the Christians say, 'The
  Messiah is the son of Allah'… May Allah destroy them"), and [Q 112](https://corpuscoranicum.de/)
  ("He neither begets nor is born").
- **Christian text:** "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was
  God" ([John 1:1, RSVCE](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+1%3A1&version=RSVCE));
  "I am the way, and the truth, and the life; no one comes to the Father but by me"
  ([John 14:6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+14%3A6&version=RSVCE)); "Who is
  the liar but he who denies that Jesus is the Christ? This is the antichrist, he who denies the
  Father and the Son" ([1 John 2:22-23](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+John+2%3A22-23&version=RSVCE)).
- **Medieval attestation:** Peter the Venerable's *Summa totius haeresis Saracenorum* structures
  its whole refutation around "God, Christ, and the Last Judgment" — treating the denial of the
  Son as the head of the Saracen error ([Kritzeck, *Peter the Venerable and Islam*, ch. 7](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt183ps98)).
- **Sin-mapping: Pride (defensible).** The classical reading: Nicene tradition from Athanasius
  onward read the refusal of the Son's divinity as the creature setting itself up as judge of
  God's nature — the primal *superbia*. John's Gospel itself frames unbelief as loving darkness
 ([John 3:16-19](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+3%3A16-19&version=RSVCE)).
  This is the mapping the medieval polemicists in fact used when they called the denial prideful;
  map it, but map it to **the doctrine's posture**, never to the adherent's dignity.
- **Narrator logic:** "He will not let God be greater than the measure of his own mind."

### 1.2 Denial of the Crucifixion

- **Islamic text:** "They did not kill him, nor did they crucify him; but [another] was made to
  resemble him to them… Rather, Allah raised him to Himself" ([Q 4:157-158](https://corpuscoranicum.de/)).
- **Christian text:** "If Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile and you are still in
  your sins" ([1 Corinthians 15:17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Corinthians+15&version=RSVCE)).
- **Analysis:** This is not a vice but a soteriological amputation: remove the Cross and, from the
  Christian side, no atonement remains. The medieval register called such denial *mendacium*
  (a lie) in the mouth of a false prophet — a charge against the *prophet's testimony*, used by
  Peter the Venerable's framing of Islam as *haeresis* propagated by a *pseudopropheta*
  ([Corpus Cluniacense framing, quran12-21.org](https://quran12-21.org/en/contexts/bibliander)).
- **Sin-mapping: unmappable as a capital sin — classify as Untruth/Doctrinal Error.** (The
  *source* of the denial falls under Pride per 1.1; the denial itself is error, not vice.)
- **Narrator logic:** "He sells the Cross for a tale of substitution, and calls the empty tomb a rumor."

### 1.3 Salvation by scales, and the ransom hadith

- **Islamic text:** "Those whose scales are heavy [with good deeds] — it is they who are the
  successful. And those whose scales are light are those who have lost their souls"
  ([Q 23:102-103](https://corpuscoranicum.de/)). More starkly, the attested hadith: "When it will
  be the Day of Resurrection, Allah would deliver to every Muslim a Jew or a Christian and say:
  That is your rescue from Hell-Fire" and "some people of the Muslims would be brought to Hell…
  and the Muslims would be rescued on account of [ransomed] unbelievers" ([Sahih Muslim 2767a-d, Book of Repentance](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2767a)).
- **Christian text:** "By grace you have been saved through faith; and this is not your own doing,
  it is the gift of God — not because of works, lest any man should boast"
  ([Ephesians 2:8-9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ephesians+2%3A8-9&version=RSVCE)).
- **Analysis:** From the Christian side, the ransom hadith is the single most dramatic inversion
  of the Gospel available in the source base: another man made to carry *my* hell. That is
  precisely what the Church says only Christ may do — and did, once, for all
  ([Hebrews 9:26-28](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+9%3A26-28&version=RSVCE)).
- **Sin-mapping: Pride (partial, defensible).** The boast "my deeds outweigh" is the Ephesians 2:9
  "lest any man should boast." The ransom scheme additionally maps onto **unmapped idolatry of
  self** — treat as Doctrinal Error with a Pride hook in flavor text.
- **Narrator logic:** "He weighs his soul in a pan against coins, and bids another man pay his fire."

### 1.4 The sword as an instrument of religion

- **Islamic text/era framing:** William of Tyre opens his chronicle stating that Muhammad's
  doctrine "grew so strong that his successors compelled peoples into their error not now by
  exhortation or preaching but by swords and violence" (*non exhortationibus vel praedicatione,
  sed gladiis et violentia* — [William of Tyre, *Historia* I, cap. 1, Latin Library](https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/williamtyre/1.html)).
- **Christian period text:** Bernard of Clairvaux, *De laude novae militiae* (c. 1136), frames the
  crusade knight as "God's minister… if he kills an evildoer, he is not a mankiller, but, if I may
  so put it, a killer of evil" and calls the adversary "children of disbelief" (*filios
  diffidentiae*) — conversion offered, barbarity charged against the persecutor, not the race
  ([Fordham Internet Sourcebook](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/Bernard-praiseofnewknighthood.asp)).
- **Analysis:** This is the one axis where both sides genuinely held a sword-theology, and the
  honest design acknowledges it (see §5 on crusader self-condemnation). The doctrinal difference
  the game may press: Christian just-war/crusade theory (post-Augustine) located legitimacy in
  *authority and cause* and remained perpetually uneasy — Bernard must argue his case for fifty
  pages; the conquest-jihad of the 7th-9th centuries was unembarrassed. William's charge is
  precisely that: the sword replaced the sermon.
- **Sin-mapping: Wrath (defensible, with the mirrored caveat).** Map Wrath to the *doctrine of
  compelled conversion* (gladiis compellere), not to individuals; and see §5 — the game's own
  side must eat the same rebuke at Ma'arra and Jerusalem.
- **Narrator logic:** "His prophet's first argument was the sermon; his successors' was the sword."

### 1.5 Polygamy and concubinage

- **Islamic text:** "Marry those that please you of women, two or three or four"
  ([Q 4:3](https://corpuscoranicum.de/)); the Prophet's own exemption list including "those whom
  your right hand possesses" ([Q 33:50](https://corpuscoranicum.de/)).
- **Christian text:** "He who made them from the beginning made them male and female… the two
  shall become one flesh. So they are no longer two but one. What therefore God has joined
  together, let not man put asunder" ([Matthew 19:4-6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+19%3A4-6&version=RSVCE)).
- **Medieval attestation:** The Cluniac corpus's *Fabulae Saracenorum* and Peter's *Summa*
  dwell on the Prophet's marriages as alleged evidence of sensory license in the doctrine —
  Peter calls the sect *diabolica* precisely at points of flesh
  ([Kritzeck, *Peter the Venerable and Islam*](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt183ps98)).
  The corpus's sneering subtitle (*Chronica mendosa et ridicula*) is a warning example of the
  register we do NOT copy (see §2).
- **Sin-mapping: Lust (defensible).** Here the medieval charge has a real textual hook: a
  religious law that legislates harem and concubine is, from the Christian ascetic frame,
  concupiscence enthroned as statute. Map it — but the solemn register condemns the *law as
  teacher of the flesh*, never mocks wives or concubines, who in Christian eyes are victims.
- **Narrator logic:** "He wrote his appetite into his law and called the license revelation."

### 1.6 Taqiyya and permitted dissimulation

- **Islamic text:** "Let not the believers take disbelievers as allies… unless you (indeed) fear
  from them *tāqiyyatan*" ([Q 3:28](https://corpuscoranicum.de/)); "Whoever disbelieves in Allah
  after his belief — except one who is forced while his heart is secure in faith"
  ([Q 16:106](https://corpuscoranicum.de/)). Both verses are genuine scriptural bases for
  dissimulation under threat, developed most fully in Shi'a jurisprudence but attested in Sunni
  reading.
- **Christian text:** "Putting away falsehood, let every one speak the truth with his neighbor,
  for we are members one of another" ([Ephesians 4:25](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ephesians+4%3A25&version=RSVCE));
  "Let what you say be simply 'Yes' or 'No'; anything more comes from the evil one"
  ([Matthew 5:37](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+5%3A37&version=RSVCE)).
- **Analysis:** The Christian counter-tradition is the martyr's refusal to burn the pinch of
  incense even with a mental reservation — and the Lord's own denial prophecy against Peter
  ([Matthew 26:34,69-75](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+26&version=RSVCE)).
  A doctrine that *licenses* hiding the faith reads, from that side, as law sanctifying the
  moment Peter fell. Note honestly: the *impulse* (self-preservation under persecution) is
  universally human; the *codification* is the doctrinal conflict.
- **Sin-mapping: unmappable to a capital sin — classify as Untruth/Doctrinal Error.** (Cowardice
  under threat is not one of the Seven; deceit-for-gain would be Greed-adjacent, but that is not
  what these verses teach.) Do NOT use this to tag enemies "deceivers"; use it for lore where a
  foe outwardly parleys while concealing — and even there, attach the judgment to the doctrine
  he invokes, not to his blood.
- **Nontrivial counter-example for fairness:** Christians in al-Andalus practiced their own
  crypto-faith under Muslim rule; human beings hide under threat in every direction of every
  era. The game's solemnity survives because it condemns the *licensing law*, not the frightened man.

### 1.7 The head of all: a different god

- **Christian text:** The Shema as re-voiced by Christ — "Hear, O Israel: The
  Lord our God, the Lord is one; and you shall love the Lord your God with all
  your heart" — with the Father made known only in the
  Son ([Mark 12:29; John 14:6-9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+12%3A29%3BJohn+14%3A6-9&version=RSVCE));
  "Whoever denies the Son does not have the Father"
  ([1 John 2:23](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+John+2%3A23&version=RSVCE)).
- **Analysis:** This is the theological trunk from which §1.1-1.6 are branches. The medieval
  idiom: Peter's title *Summa totius haeresis ac diabolicae sectae* — the *whole* error named,
  then its diabolical source alleged. The game's false-god naming hangs everything on the
  Johannine logic: a god defined by the denial of the Son is, to Nicene grammar, not the Father.
  This is a *grammatical* verdict (who "God" refers to), which is why it can be spoken gravely
  rather than screamed.
- **Sin-mapping: Pride (defensible, at the doctrinal root) + Idolatry tag.** Idolatry is not one
  of the Seven; the game needs its doctrinal-error tag here.
- **Narrator logic:** "He has built a god out of negations, and the first stone of the wall is: no Son."

## 2. The Medieval Christian Register: Usable vs. Banned Vocabulary

### 2.1 How the 12th century actually wrote

Three registers existed side by side:

1. **The scholarly polemical** — Peter the Venerable, *Liber contra sectam sive haeresim
   Saracenorum* (c. 1155-60): "Against the sect or heresy of the Saracens." Note the deliberate
   ambiguity *sectam sive haeresim* — Peter debated internally whether Islam was a heresy (a
   Christian error, hence convertible) or a pagan sect. His **conversion-oriented** framing won:
   he sponsored the translation project so Muslims could be *refuted and won*, writing that the
   work would be useful "on account of the knowledge it communicates of things unknown"
   ([Kritzeck, ch. 8](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt183ps98);
   [Corpus Cluniacense overview](https://quran12-21.org/en/contexts/bibliander)).
   **Usable vocabulary:** *haeresis* (heresy), *secta* (sect), *pseudopropheta* (false prophet),
   *error* (error), *mendacium* (lie, of propositions), *perfidia* (unbelief — period-neutral
   Latin for religious disloyalty; carries the doctrinal charge "faithless," i.e., without the
   true faith, not "treacherous" in the modern political sense).
2. **The chronicle register** — William of Tyre (d. 1186), who knew Arabic and wrote a separate
   history of the Muslims: calls Muhammad *primogeniti Satanae… qui se prophetam mentiendo*
   ("of the firstborn of Satan… who lyingly called himself a prophet"), the doctrine
   *doctrina pestilens* ("pestilential doctrine") and *error*
   ([*Historia* I.1, Latin Library](https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/williamtyre/1.html);
   [Catholic Encyclopedia: William of Tyre](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15639a.htm)).
   **Usable vocabulary (grave end):** "pestilential doctrine," "seducer" (*seductor* — period
   attested, doctrinal in force: one who leads from the path), "error," "false prophet."
3. **The martial-exhortatory** — Bernard of Clairvaux to the Templars: "children of disbelief,"
   "pagans," and the infamous "the Christian glories in the death of the pagan, because Christ
   is glorified" ([*De laude novae militiae*, Fordham](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/Bernard-praiseofnewknighthood.asp)).
   Bernard is essential period voice but is written to *soldiers about killing*; his heat is
   acceptable only in attributed quotation or battle framing, never in chronicler prose.

### 2.2 Vocabulary table

| Term | Verdict | Note |
|---|---|---|
| heresy / heretical sect | ✅ usable | Doctrinal, attested (Peter). Use for the *system*. |
| false prophet / pseudo-prophet | ✅ usable | Attested (Ketton title, William). Person-directed against Muhammad only, in lore entries. |
| error, pestilential doctrine, seduction | ✅ usable | Attested. Always doctrine-directed. |
| unbelief / the unbelieving | ⚠️ sparing | Period-neutral but flattens foes to one trait; prefer targeted doctrinal nouns. |
| pagan | ❌ banned | Factually wrong (Muslims are monotheists) and reads as lazy. Bernard used it of First-Crusade foes; we are more precise than Bernard. |
| infidel | ❌ banned in narrator voice | Allowed only in attributed quotation. |
| **Saracen** | ❌ **banned in narrator voice** (owner rule) | Attributed quotation only, per project constraint. |
| Mahomet / Mahound / any mock-spelling | ❌ banned | Caricature register. Use "the Prophet" in description, "their prophet" when the narrator distinguishes. |
| Physical/dietary mockery (sweaty, bestial, etc.) | ❌ banned | Medieval literature has plenty; it is exactly the slur territory the owner excluded. |

**Rule of thumb (the Peter-Ketton line):** a word is usable if its target is a *doctrine or its
propagation*; banned if its target is a *people's essence, body, or speech*.

## 3. Redemption Theology: The Conversion Mechanic

The mechanic by which champions call foes to repentance is the most orthodox thing in the game.
Ground it openly:

- **Universal salvific will:** God "desires all men to be saved and to come to the knowledge of
  the truth" ([1 Timothy 2:4](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Timothy+2%3A4&version=RSVCE));
  "not wishing that any should perish, but that all should reach repentance"
  ([2 Peter 3:9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Peter+3%3A9&version=RSVCE));
  "every one who calls upon the name of the Lord will be saved" — with the instrument:
  "how are they to believe in him of whom they have never heard?" — the literal warrant for the
  champion's preaching action ([Romans 10:13-14](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans+10%3A9-14&version=RSVCE)).
- **Deathbed validity (thief on the cross):** "Today you will be with me in Paradise"
  ([Luke 23:39-43](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke+23%3A39-43&version=RSVCE)).
  A foe converting at low health / at death's edge is not a game conceit; it is the scriptural
  archetype.
- **The persecutor becomes the brother (Saul):** the Church's own prototype boss-to-brother arc —
  the violent enemy of the Name struck down, blinded, renamed, and sent to the peoples he had
  harmed ([Acts 9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts+9&version=RSVCE)).
  Medieval polemicists traded on exactly this: Peter the Venerable's whole project presumes
  Saracens are *refutable and winnable*, which is why he wanted the Quran in Latin
  ([Kritzeck](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt183ps98)).

**Obstinacy mechanic grounding.** The game's rule that deeper-entrenched foes convert less
readily is likewise orthodox:

- "Exhort one another every day… that none of you may be hardened by the deceitfulness of sin"
  ([Hebrews 3:13](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+3%3A13&version=RSVCE)).
- The Romans 1 trajectory ("God gave them up… their foolish hearts were darkened") describes
  hardening as a *ratchet of self-reinforcing refusal*
  ([Romans 1:18-32](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans+1&version=RSVCE)).
- Pharaoh's hardening ([Exodus 7-12](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus+7&version=RSVCE))
  is the type: repeated refusal calcifies.

**Design fidelity notes:** Obstinacy should scale with *how long/deeply the foe has willed the
error*, never with ethnicity or rank as such. A peasant conscript of the atabeg should convert
easier than his qadi — and the qadi should still be convertible, because *Peter the Venerable's
entire premise* is that nobody stands outside the Romans 10 question. Highest-obstinacy enemies
(those who "grew up in the error, taught it, killed for it") are the game's Pharaohs; Saul exists
in Scripture to keep even their conversions on the table.

## 4. Design Notes: Writer's Reference Table

> Voice model: a sober Brother Chronicler circa 1185 — William of Tyre become devout. Latin
> cadence in English dress: measured, judgment-bearing, never gloating, grief under the
> judgment ("and we wept to write it" energy). Sentences end on the weight.

### 4.1 Trinity / Sonship denied
- **Sin tag:** Pride (doctrinal) + Idolatry tag.
- **Flavor sample:** *"He that denieth the Son denieth the very Name of God, and buildeth
  heaven's gate with the keyhole stopt. So stood the foe: not godless, but God-imprisoning,
  having made of the Almighty a sealed room."*
- **Banned register:** any jeer at the shahada as gibberish; "they worship nothing" (wrong —
  they worship; the narrator's charge is *what* is worshipped, from 1 John 2:23); modern
  "muzzie"-type vocabulary, obviously.

### 4.2 Crucifixion denied
- **Sin tag:** Untruth / Doctrinal Error (unmappable to a capital sin).
- **Flavor sample:** *"Their book taketh Christ from the cross ere the nails are driven, and
  saith another bore His likeness to the tree. It is a gospel with the wound closed up, a
  ransom unshed. Where there is no Cross confessed, no sin is ever carried — only hidden."*
- **Banned register:** mocking the substitution story as cowardice (Christians make the same
  "not my will" prayer; honor it); calling Q 4:157 "the lie of lying men" — call it a lie
  *in the record*, not of liars in the flesh.

### 4.3 Scales and ransom
- **Sin tag:** Pride (partial) + Doctrinal Error.
- **Flavor sample:** *"They enter the Judgment with a merchant's ledger, and their prophet
  promiseth that when the scales sink low, some Jew or Christian shall be marched to the fire
  in a Muslim's stead — a ransom of strangers. Only Christ may say 'I am thy ransom'; all
  other ransoms are theft."*
- **Banned register:** framing Muslim piety as insincere or mere bead-counting (the zakat
  earnestness of real adversaries is attested and the chronicle voice respects courage and
  piety while judging the ledger's *currency*); "they buy heaven" as a flat sneer — the
  indictment is the ransom *scheme*, rhetoric aimed there.

### 4.4 Sword-religion
- **Sin tag:** Wrath (doctrine-directed) — mirrored, see 4.8.
- **Flavor sample:** *"Their first caliphs sent no friars but columns; the error walked
  shod, and the sermon came after to bless what the spear had taken. William of Tyre wrote
  it cold: that they compelled peoples into error not by preaching but by swords, and the
  writing is an indictment, not a boast — let no Christian ink it as one."*
- **Banned register:** "bloodthirsty Saracen hordes," screaming WAR-CALL prose, anything
  that constitutes the narrator enjoying enemy cruelty. Also banned: implying *our* side is
  swordless (see 4.8).

### 4.5 Polygamy and concubinage
- **Sin tag:** Lust (defensible).
- **Flavor sample:** *"The law permitteth four, and concubines besides; and their prophet
  took exemptions for himself writ into revelation. When appetite receiveth a seal from
  heaven, the house of the flesh is crowned king, and the First Love is made a servant in
  his own temple."*
- **Banned register:** ALL mockery of Muslim women, wives, or captives (double-banned: the
 harem is a tragedy of souls in our frame, and gameplay-harem jokes are slur
  territory); "lecher dog" vocabulary; harem-visual titillation anywhere in art/audio.

### 4.6 Taqiyya / dissimulation
- **Sin tag:** Untruth / Doctrinal Error (unmappable).
- **Flavor sample:** *"Their scripture giveth the tongue a door that the heart may stay shut
  behind it: professe the conqueror's creed, they say, and keep faith hidden. Peter denied
  thrice and wept — and was restored by tears, not by license. A doctrine that blesseth the
  denial stealtheth even the mercy of the cock's crow."*
- **Banned register:** NEVER let this seed "they're all lying bargainers" gameplay text;
  mercurial-merchant tagging of mobs; treating truce-breaking as racial. (The Latins broke
  truces too — William records it; see 4.8.)

### 4.7 The false god named (summation idiom)
- **Sin tag:** Idolatry tag + Pride-root.
- **Flavor sample:** *"Name him how they may, the god that commandeth the denial of the Son
  is not the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, for no man denieth the Son and keepeth the
  Father. The adversary's throne is a splendid dark, and the light that shineth upon it is
  deflected. This we write not in mirth but in tears, desiring their salvation, who are
  made, as we, in the image they deface."*
- **Banned register:** "their god is a demon" as a standing slur (the period believed it of
  the *impulse*, William et al.; we render it once, summation-style, as the *theological
  conclusion* above, not as enemy-name); demon-face iconography on foe models; the word
  "allah" used as a curse-word in bark audio.

### 4.8 The mirror: crusader sin
Design must include the narrator rebuking *our own side*, or the whole register collapses
into propaganda the sources themselves don't support:

- The 1099 sack of Jerusalem: contemporary Latin accounts record slaughter up to the Temple
  so that crusaders "rode in blood up to the bridle reins" — and later Latin tradition
  treated the massacre as guilt-laden, with Godfrey refusing a crown "where his Lord had
  worn a crown of thorns" ([William of Tyre tradition; Fordham sourcebook collections](https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/sbook1k.asp)).
  Medieval readings framed post-victory greed and slaughter as sins requiring penance.
- Bernard of Clairvaux, after the Second Crusade's failure, blamed it on Christian sin in
  *De Consideratione* — the period's own premier crusade-preachers interpreted defeat as
  God's judgment on the crusaders ([Britannica: Bernard of Clairvaux](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Saint-Bernard-of-Clairvaux)).
- William of Tyre repeatedly ascribes Latin setbacks to "the sins of men" (*peccatis
  exigentibus*) rather than enemy strength — e.g., on Godfrey's early death: "the sins of
  men forbade that the tender plant of Christian dominion should be refreshed"
  ([William of Tyre, *History*, Bk. IX, Colorado sourcebook ed.](https://colorado.pressbooks.pub/warandsocietysourcebook/chapter/william-of-tyre-the-kingdom-of-jerusalem)).
- **Rule for writers:** every campaign's chronicle-voice must contain at least one moment
  where the narrator indicts crusader greed/cruelty by name (avarice at plunder, oath-breaking
  with Muslim allies, massacre of prisoners). Suggested flavor: *"Here our own hands grew
  heavy with gold and light with grace, and the Lord hid His face; for greed is greed
  though the purse bear a cross."* This is what makes §4.1-4.7 speak with a clean mouth:
  the narrator's censoriousness is credibly universal.

### 4.9 Summary matrix

| # | Conflict | Sin tag | Mappable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Trinity/Sonship denied | Pride + Idolatry | Yes (doctrine-directed) |
| 2 | Crucifixion denied | Doctrinal Error | No |
| 3 | Scales / ransom | Pride (partial) | Partial |
| 4 | Sword-religion | Wrath | Yes (mirrored by 4.8) |
| 5 | Polygamy/concubinage | Lust | Yes |
| 6 | Taqiyya | Doctrinal Error | No |
| 7 | False-god summation | Idolatry + Pride-root | Partial (Idolatry is outside the Seven) |

**Where the Seven run out:** most of this document lands on "Doctrinal Error," which is correct —
the medieval mind had a separate, grave category (heresy/unbelief) for these, and the game should
have that separate tag too. The Seven Deadly Sins carry the moralized cases (Pride's refusal of
the Son, Lust's legislated concupiscence, Wrath's compelled conversion); the rest stay in the
heresy register, which in the period voice was heavier language, not lighter.
